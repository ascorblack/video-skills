# Interface recording

Every rule is taken from the lessons of past videos; the source is in square brackets (legend at the bottom).
Where the sources disagree, both pieces of advice are given, with a note on which case each applies to.

## Short rules

1. Shoot frame by frame, drive the browser by virtual time: advanced a frame — took a frame. A real-time screen recording tears frames. [Video] [3D]
2. Shoot only a demo stand with invented data; before recording, page through every screen with your eyes. [Video] [3D]
3. The stand must be consistent with itself: two screens that read one fact say the same thing. [Video]
4. The cursor goes to the target, not past it; draw it on the page from the same events that go into the application. [Video] [3D]
5. Put marks right during recording and hang captions and the camera on marks, not on seconds. [Video]
6. Give margins of silence at the start and at the end of a take [Video]; in the second language version the intro and the tail are of the same length as in the original [3D].
7. Look at every take with a sheet of 4–6 frames. [Video]
8. Any wait of the recorder — with a timeout; kill background processes by pid. [Video]
9. Show the recording up to the visible result of the action; a push-in — onto a whole block, not into a button. [Video] [3D]
10. Real interface or drawn — depends on the video: a promo about a program shoots the real one [Video], the stylized popular-science video redrew it [Popsci]. See `framing.md`.
